1. Intended result and non-negotiable distinctions

Design one U.S. parent company capable of holding equity in authorized subsidiaries and operating companies across the IMPERIAL Core ecosystem, raising third-party capital, attracting professional teams and maintaining founder-led long-term architectural governance.

• Authorized shares are a ceiling in the charter, not issued shares, not assets, not market value.
• Issued/outstanding shares are legally granted and recorded in the stock ledger after proper approvals and consideration.
• Reserved shares are only an internal planning designation or board-approved plan reservation, not an investor’s or employee’s ownership.
• Voting power may differ from economic ownership under a valid dual-class charter.
• Owner technical architectural authority does not replace fiduciary duties of directors, applicable stockholder voting rights, contractual rights of investors, securities laws, or judicial oversight.
• Corporate shares are not Polygon tokens and do not automatically impart equity to NDB, IMPERIUM, or other token holders.

3. Founder-control provisions proposed for drafting by Delaware counsel

1. Class B high vote: 10 votes for each B; Class A one vote, subject to mandatory class-specific votes under law.
2. Economic parity: one B and one A share have the same ordinary economic rights unless subsequent corporate actions lawfully change the capital structure.
3. Transfer-conversion: founder B ordinarily converts 1:1 into A when transferred to a non-permitted holder; define narrow permitted family, estate and controlled-trust transfers only after tax and securities-law review.
4. Optional sunset: discuss a founder ownership threshold and/or time-based sunset with counsel and investors. Do not promise perpetual high-vote control. A time or ownership sunset can make the design more acceptable to counterparties.
5. No additional super-voting issuance without special review: charter/shareholder agreements should address approvals to authorize more B or another high-vote series, subject to DGCL amendment rules and separate class votes.
6. Board: initially one director (founder), as permitted by Delaware law; at financing, potentially expand to three (e.g., two founder-aligned and one agreed independent) only if negotiated legally with other shareholders. Directors owe fiduciary duties to the corporation and shareholders, not personally to the founder.
7. Founder title: Founder, CEO and Chief Systems Architect proposed at formation, subject to correct corporate appointment and board oversight. «Final Architectural Decision Authority» is an internal engineering-policy role, limited by law, board authority and investor contracts.
8. Founder-specific reserved matters: major sale of core IP, incorporation of a financing subsidiary, approval of certain related-party transactions, and share-class changes can be subject to a legally drafted matrix of board/shareholder approvals. A blanket unilateral veto should not be asserted without a valid legal basis.
9. Conflicts: disinterested board review where required, documented recusals, arms-length licensing and approval workflows for owner-related token entities.
10. Information and records: cap table, board minutes, stock ledger, rights register, resolutions, audit evidence and material contracts kept in a permissioned corporate repository.

Limits of voting control: With 8m B = 80m votes, 20m newly authorized and issued one-vote A would give founder 80/(80+20)=80% ordinary voting power; 100m new one-vote A would reduce it to 44.44%. These quantities exceed today’s 10m-share authorization and would require legally approved amendments. Investor vetoes, class votes and director fiduciary duties can further constrain outcomes even while founder has a majority of ordinary votes.

4. Founder-share terms and tax-sensitive choices

The founder’s historical work on the ecosystem must be inventoried before deciding whether the founder shares are immediately vested or subject to reverse vesting. A standard employee-style four-year vesting schedule should not be imposed blindly on previously created founder IP or work.

If actual restricted shares subject to a substantial risk of forfeiture are issued in exchange for services, obtain tax advice about a timely IRC §83(b) election. The general deadline is 30 days from the transfer date, not from incorporation; Form 15620 is available from the IRS. Relevance and U.S. filing mechanics for a founder resident outside the U.S. require individual review. If unrestricted shares are issued, §83(b) may be inapplicable.

Document: board approval under DGCL §§152–153; Founder Stock Purchase/Subscription Agreement; applicable restricted-stock/reverse-vesting instrument; proof of payment or legally accepted property; Stock Ledger; Section 83(b) paperwork if applicable; assignment/license of specifically enumerated IP; conflict-of-interest review.

7. Regulatory and tax checklist as of 2026-10-24

Delaware

• A nonresident may form a Delaware corporation. A Delaware registered agent is required.
• The Delaware state fee schedule revised August 1, 2026 lists a $109 base domestic incorporation fee, variable with authorized stock, and $50 for an optional certified copy. Seek an exact fee quote through the state calculator for this particular two-class par-value charter; do not treat $109 as an unconditional final bill.
• Delaware corporate annual report + franchise tax are due on or before March 1 for the preceding year. Official state pages show a $50 annual report fee and two alternative franchise tax methods: minimum $175 using Authorized Shares versus minimum $400 using Assumed Par Value Capital Method. For 10m authorized shares, the Authorized Shares method may yield a very large figure; assess the alternative method using actual gross assets and issued shares. A $400 annual state franchise tax is not guaranteed beyond the statutory minimum. Late filing penalty and interest apply.
• Registered-agent service, legal drafting, tax-accounting and other jurisdictions’ taxes are additional.

United States federal

• IRS EIN issuance is free. For non-U.S. responsible parties with no U.S. legal residence/principal office, the online method generally is unavailable; use the permitted international phone, fax or mail process. IRS Form SS-4 instructions specify when foreign/N/A may be entered if the natural-person responsible party lacks and is ineligible for a SSN/ITIN.
• U.S. C-corporation federal Form 1120 and applicable filing/payment duties persist even with little operating activity; obtain CPA guidance.
• A 25%-or-more foreign-owned U.S. corporation may have Form 5472 obligations if it has reportable transactions with foreign or domestic related parties. The founder’s contributions, intercompany activity and IP must be reviewed.
• The SEC requires an effective securities registration or an available exemption for offers/sales of shares. Founder issuance must be documented under an appropriate exemption; employee awards may use Rule 701 if conditions are met; private fundraising may qualify for Rule 506(b) or alternatives. Rule 506(b) generally bans broad public investor solicitation and requires Form D after the first sale.
• Under FinCEN’s March 2025 interim final rule, U.S.-created domestic companies are exempt from CTA BOI reporting as of the referenced guidance. Recheck at the time of formation; this does not eliminate bank KYC or other disclosures.

Ukraine, conditional on individual’s tax residency

• If founder is a tax resident of Ukraine, majority control in the U.S. company may trigger Ukrainian controlled foreign company (КІК) rules and personal reporting obligations. Ukrainian tax authority guidance (August 7, 2026) explains a 60-day CFC acquisition-of-control notification for covered events. Residency, actual control and date of relevant event require a Ukrainian tax specialist’s determination.
• U.S.–Ukraine treaty applicability, dividend treatment, cross-border compensation and IP migrations require individualized advice; do not assume U.S. incorporation replaces Ukrainian tax duties or establishes U.S. immigration/residence status.

9. Risks and decisions needing counsel sign-off

• A two-class 10m charter with all founder Class B shares issued on day one preserves a clean precise denominator but makes future venture financing more complicated than a standard single-class startup charter; compare investor appetite against governance priorities.
• Investors may demand preferred economic rights, protective provisions, board seats or conversion/sunset provisions. An ordinary 97.56% voting majority does not nullify lawful minority or class rights.
• Founder liquidity/economic ownership and corporate valuation are unrelated to an illustrative token-wallet value; there is no verified $50bn company valuation.
• If a suitable U.S. corporation already legally exists, incorporation anew could be unnecessary or counterproductive. Verify first; do not automatically create duplicate entities.
• A legal holding company can own subsidiaries through issued shares or other instruments, but current IMPERIAL Core products and tokens have not been shown to be owned by the proposed company.
